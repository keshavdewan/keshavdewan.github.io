## Project Overview

Created for the **Onyx Data & ZoomCharts Challenge**, this analytical project models customer satisfaction and loyalty analysis for OmniRetail, an omnichannel electronics and smart-home retailer operating online and physical stores.

## Business Problem

Leadership wanted to understand what distinguishes highly loyal customers from dissatisfied buyers—specifically whether customer support interactions, pricing, packaging, product variety or regional store experiences drive satisfaction scores.

## Data Sources & Technologies

- **Dataset:** 2024 customer satisfaction dataset containing 120 rows and 12 columns (customer group A/B/C, satisfaction score 1–10, age, gender, city/state coordinates, purchase history, support contact indicator, loyalty tier and primary satisfaction factor).
- **Technologies:** Power BI, DAX, ZoomCharts and Microsoft Excel.

## Technical Approach & Key Questions

The model and DAX measures were structured to answer four core business questions:
- How does satisfaction vary between customer groups (high-frequency Group A, moderate Group B and low-frequency Group C)?
- Does customer support interaction correlate with lower or higher satisfaction scores?
- Which satisfaction factors (such as Price, Product Variety or Packaging) influence customer loyalty?
- Are there geographic patterns across U.S. cities and states?

## Implementation

- Prepared and modeled the 2024 feedback dataset in Power BI with clean segment groupings and DAX KPI measures.
- Built interactive ZoomCharts drill-down charts and geographic views enabling users to slice satisfaction scores by support contact, loyalty level and demographic segment.

## Key Findings & Results

- Surfaced how primary drivers such as Price, Product Variety and Packaging vary across customer frequency groups and regions.
- Documented the full methodology and analytical walkthrough in a published Medium article alongside the GitHub repository.

## Challenges & Learnings

- With a compact 120-row dataset, avoiding over-segmentation is critical—grouping visuals around clear business questions keeps findings interpretable rather than noisy.
